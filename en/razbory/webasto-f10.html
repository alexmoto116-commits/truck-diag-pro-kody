Webasto F10: the heater overheated - where to start

In short. F10 is the heater's overheat lock-out, not an engine fault. The factory requires finding and removing the cause of the overheating: first check the air intake, the exhaust and the warm-air outlet.

Webasto, code F10. What is it? Overheating: the lock-out has triggered, the heater overheated. This is the Air Top 2000 ST; on other models F10 means something else.

First check the air intake and the exhaust: no snow, ice or dirt, and the pipe is not crushed. The warm-air outlet into the cab must not be blocked. Inspect the fuel system for leaks. If the air and exhaust are clear but the overheating repeats, go to a workshop: heat exchanger cleaning and a check of the overheat sensor.

Which model this is about

F10 in this sense applies to the Webasto Air Top 2000 ST and matches service codes 06 and 17. On other models F10 may mean something else, so first find out the model.

Order of actions

  1. Inspect the air intake and the exhaust.
  2. Make sure the warm-air outlet into the cab is not blocked.
  3. Inspect the fuel system for leaks.
  4. Remove what you found and switch the heater on again.

This is not an engine fault, but the factory wants the cause of the overheating found and removed, not just the heater restarted.

When to see a workshop

If air and exhaust are clear and the overheating repeats, the heat exchanger needs cleaning and the overheat sensor checking.

Questions and answers

What does F10 mean on a Webasto timer?

On the Air Top 2000 ST the overheat lock-out has triggered: the heater overheated and stopped.

Is it an engine fault?

No, it is the heater's overheat lock-out.

What should I check first?

The air intake and the exhaust (snow, ice, dirt, a crushed pipe), then the warm-air outlet into the cab and the fuel system for leaks.

When is a workshop needed for F10?

If air and exhaust are clear and the overheating repeats: heat exchanger cleaning and an overheat sensor check.
