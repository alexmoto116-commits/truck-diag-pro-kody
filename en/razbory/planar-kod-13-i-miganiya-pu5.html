Planar: why code 13 and 13 blinks are different faults

In short. Code 13 on a display controller matches two blinks of the PU-5, while 13 blinks is code 35, found only on the Planar 8DM. The numbers in the two systems do not match, so first find out exactly what you are looking at.

The video is in Russian; below is an English translation of the narration.

The Planar will not start. Two flashes on the controller mean code 13: it failed to start in two attempts. The glow plug heats up, but there is no flame. The PU-5 blinks twice; on a display controller it shows 13.

First: the fuel - level, season, fuel line, pump. Second: clean the air intake and the exhaust. Third: the glow plug - clean the hole and the screen - and the voltage.

Two systems, one heater

Repair code: a number from the repair manual table, shown on a display controller such as the PU-22: 01, 02, 13, 29, 78 and so on.

PU-5 blinks: this controller has no display; a red LED blinks the needed number of times and pauses. Any button resets the signal.

The same failure carries different numbers in the two systems. The correspondence is given in the Autoterm user manual.

Three traps follow from the table. Two blinks on the PU-5 are not code 02 but code 13. Four blinks are not 04 but the glow plug (09). And 13 blinks are not code 13 but code 35: flame-out caused by a voltage dip, present only on the Planar 8DM.

The most common codes

13: the heater did not start. Two automatic start attempts failed. The manual says to check the fuel (is there any, and is it suitable for the temperature), the fuel line for leaks and blockage, the air intake and exhaust, the blower and whether the glow plug heats. Checking the plug and the voltage at the unit counts as repair work.

29 and 78: flame-out. The flame went out during operation. Causes: too little fuel, a faulty pump or flame indicator; for 78 an air bubble in the fuel system is added. On the PU-5 both codes give three blinks.

04 and 06: temperature sensor in the unit. It is built into the control unit and is not replaced separately; it has no wiring. The only cure is replacing the unit, which is service work.

What to check yourself

  • fuel in the tank and whether it suits the season;
  • exhaust and air intake: the passage must be clear;
  • fuel line: leaks and tight clamps;
  • blower: no sticking and no foreign objects.

When to see a workshop

  • code 04 or 06: the control unit must be replaced;
  • 13 or 29 repeat after the fuel, exhaust and fuel line were checked;
  • the plug, fuel pump or flame indicator needs testing;
  • you could not find the cause from the list above.

Questions and answers

Are two blinks on the PU-5 code 02?

No. Two blinks correspond to repair code 13 (failed to start after two attempts). Code 02 on the PU-5 is twelve blinks.

What do three blinks on the PU-5 mean?

Flame-out during operation: in the repair table that is codes 29 and 08 (and 78 with air in the fuel system). There is no code 03 in the repair table.

Can the temperature sensor be replaced separately for code 04 or 06?

No, it is built into the control unit. The whole unit is replaced, and that is service work.

Where do 13 blinks come from?

That is code 35, flame-out due to a voltage dip, and only on the Planar 8DM. It has nothing to do with code 13.
