J1587/J1708 codes: MID, PID, SID, FMI - what they mean

27 pages

Older American and European trucks (and some Volvo FH and FM before EURO 5) report faults not as an SPN and FMI pair but as a triple MID + PID or SID + FMI under the SAE J1587 standard (J1708 bus). The MID says which unit reports (128 engine, 130 transmission, 136 brakes and ABS, 140 instrument cluster, 144 the vehicle ECU at Volvo). The PID is a parameter (pressure, temperature, speed), the SID a subsystem (a sensor, an actuator, a data link). The FMI is the failure type: open, short, value too high or too low.

How to read an entry

Example: MID 130 SID 231 FMI 2 - the transmission unit (130), the SAE J1939 data link (SID 231), erratic or intermittent data (FMI 2). It is not a gearbox failure but a record of an unstable CAN link. Look for a poor contact or an open wire in the J1939 harness.

Other MIDs in the standard

The other MIDs (several dozen: turbocharger 129, engine retarder 173, parking heater 224 and so on) are listed in the standard itself. There are no pages for them: they differ most between makers.

Which codes exist and how they are labelled differs between makers. The pages in this section rely on the SAE J1587 standard and Volvo tables (MID 128, 130, 136, 140, 144). On Mack, Freightliner, International, Detroit, Kenworth and Peterbilt the main MIDs and SIDs (including SID 231) match the standard, but check the code against the manual for your truck.
