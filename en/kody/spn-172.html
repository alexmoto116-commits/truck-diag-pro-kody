SPN 172 — Intake air temperature

Safe to driveEnough to reach the yard

J1939 code SPN 172. Below — how the fault is worded at the factory by Isuzu, KAMAZ, MAZ, SITRAK and YaMZ, and what the second half of the code (FMI) means in the standard.

How to check it yourself

The intake air temperature sensor — before the turbo, in the air filter housing or on the duct after it. Often it is combined with the air flow sensor (SPN 132).

What it should read

Resistance of a typical NTC sensor at 20 °Cabout 2…3 kΩ
The same at 80 °Cabout 300 Ω — more precisely per the factory table
Voltage at the harness connector with the sensor unpluggedabout 5 V

Check order

  1. FMI 3, 4: unplug the connector and measure the sensor resistance. An open sensor or wire is seen by the ECU as −40 °C frost (voltage high, FMI 3), a short as severe overheating (FMI 4). With the connector unplugged, the harness contacts should show about 5 V.
  2. Compare the diagnostics reading on a cold engine that has stood overnight with other temperatures: air, oil, coolant. All should be close to the outside temperature; one lagging 10 °C or more is suspect.
  3. Reads much warmer than outside while driving — the air is not coming from outside: the intake hose is torn off, and the engine is drawing hot air from under the cab.
  4. If the sensor is shared with the air flow meter and SPN 132 is present — the connector and the supply are shared.

Common causes — from most to least frequent

  1. Connector or wires.
  2. Torn-off or cracked air intake hose.
  3. The sensor itself.

Can I keep driving

Safe to drive. Below — how much time you have and what happens if you keep going.

What happens if you keep driving

Time you have: Enough to reach the yard

Boost is not reaching its target: turbo, intercooler or intake. The engine compensates with mixture and runs hotter than usual.

You will lose pull, burn more fuel and add overheating on long climbs. If you hear a whine or a whistle from the turbo, stop — the wheel is breaking up.

This is an electrical fault: an open, a short or a bad contact in the sensor circuit. The value it measures is most likely fine. You can drive. But until the circuit is repaired this system runs blind and cannot warn you about a real failure.

That said, the manufacturer flags this code as critical — if in doubt, it is safer to stop and check on the spot.

What to check on the spot

Inspect the air filter and charge-air hoses: leaks and cracks produce exactly these codes.
