SPN 168 — Battery voltage

Safe to driveTo the nearest safe stop

J1939 code SPN 168. Below — how the fault is worded at the factory by Cummins ISB, DAF, Detroit Diesel, Dongfeng, International, KAMAZ, KAMAZ-5490, MAZ, Mercedes-Benz, Renault Trucks, SITRAK, YaMZ and ZF AS-Tronic, and what the second half of the code (FMI) means in the standard.

How to check it yourself

The ECU measures the voltage at its own power input. This code means the ECU sees the wrong voltage: the cause is in the batteries, the alternator, or the wires and grounds between them and the ECU.

What it should read

Two charged 12 V batteries, engine off25.2…25.6 V
Engine running, alternator charging27.5…28.8 V
During crankingnot below 18 V
Difference between the battery terminals and the ECU input under loadnot more than 0.5 V

Check order

  1. Measure at the battery terminals with the engine off, then with it running.
  2. Above 29.5 V with the engine running — alternator regulator. Below 27 V — alternator, belt, or the wire from the alternator.
  3. Battery voltage is normal but the ECU sees less: measure at the ECU power connector with a load on (headlights, heater fan). A difference of more than 0.5 V means poor contact: the battery terminals, the ECU ground, the engine-to-frame ground, the power fuse.
  4. The code appears only when cranking — check the batteries with a load tester or meter: a sagging battery drops the voltage and the ECU restarts.
  5. If several ECUs have this code at once, the cause is common: batteries, alternator or the main ground, not the ECU.

Common causes — from most to least frequent

  1. Corroded or loosely tightened battery terminals.
  2. Engine-to-frame ground or ECU ground.
  3. Worn or discharged batteries.
  4. Alternator regulator, brushes, belt.
  5. ECU power fuse or relay with burnt contacts.

Can I keep driving

Safe to drive. Below — how much time you have and what happens if you keep going.

What happens if you keep driving

Time you have: To the nearest safe stop

The electrical system is not holding voltage. While the engine runs the alternator carries it; shut it off and you may not restart.

Next the lights go, then modules start dropping out and false codes appear all over the truck. Get to somewhere with help without shutting the engine down.

That said, the manufacturer flags this code as critical — if in doubt, it is safer to stop and check on the spot.

What to check on the spot

Check the battery terminals and earth strap — corrosion and loose clamps throw false codes all over the truck. Running voltage should be 27–29 V.
