Faults by symptom

No code, but the truck is not behaving right? Start with the symptom — below is what usually causes it, which codes confirm it, and whether you can make it in.

Where to start

  • Check Engine light is on — A yellow light means the electronics found a fault and logged a code
  • AdBlue (DEF) error — More than half of AdBlue errors come from an empty tank or the wrong fluid
  • Power dropped, the truck will not pull — Torque derate is not a fault by itself — it is a protection response: the electronics cut power because they found a problem
  • Low oil pressure — This is the most urgent of the common faults
  • Engine overheating — Overheating rarely comes alone — a power derate and a protective shutdown usually follow
  • Clogged particulate filter (DPF) — The filter clogs when the truck spends a lot of time idling or driving in the city — regeneration never gets a chance to run
  • Water in the fuel, clogged filter — In winter, diesel waxing adds to the problem
  • Power dropped, errors across the whole truck — If the scanner throws a dozen codes from different systems at once, it is almost never ten separate faults — it is the power supply or the bus
  • Engine will not start — The starter cranks but the engine will not catch — that is one thing; the starter does not turn at all — that is another
  • Engine stalls while driving — Almost always this is fuel delivery: an air leak, a clogged filter, a failed lift pump
  • Engine running rough, misfires — The electronics count misfires per cylinder, so the code names the culprit directly
  • Black exhaust smoke — Black smoke means too much fuel or not enough air
  • White exhaust smoke — White vapor on a cold start is normal
  • Blue smoke, oil in the exhaust — Bluish smoke means oil is entering the combustion chamber
  • Fuel consumption has gone up — Consumption rises when the engine is not running in its intended range: not enough air, a leaking injector, a clogged particulate filter
  • DPF regeneration will not run — Regeneration will not start if the truck idles a lot or the system cannot raise exhaust temperature
  • AdBlue not pumping, dosing error — The pump module is not building pressure or cannot hold it: a blocked line, crystallized DEF, a failed heater
  • NOx sensor error — There are usually two NOx sensors — upstream and downstream of the catalyst — and the control unit compares them
  • Alternator is not charging — With the engine running, the on-board network should read 27–29 V
  • Air pressure will not build — Slow build-up or falling pressure points to a leak, the compressor, or the air dryer
  • ABS light is on — The wheel speed sensor is the usual culprit — excess air gap, dirt, a damaged tone ring
  • Parking brake will not release — Spring brake chambers release the shoes with air pressure, so the first thing to check is whether the circuit has built full pressure
  • Gears will not shift — The automated manual gearbox drops into limp-home mode and leaves you with one or two gears, or neutral
  • Clutch is slipping — RPM climbs but speed does not — the discs are not holding torque
  • Retarder error — The retarder shuts itself off when the oil overheats or it loses communication with the control unit
  • Overheats under load — If the temperature climbs only on grades and drops back on level ground, the problem is usually heat rejection: the fan viscous clutch, a clogged radiator, the intercooler
  • Tachograph error — The tachograph broadcasts speed and distance over the bus, so its fault pulls in codes from other systems too — from cruise control to the speed limiter
  • Engine brake not working — The engine (compression) brake shuts itself off if the exhaust flap or its actuator is faulty
